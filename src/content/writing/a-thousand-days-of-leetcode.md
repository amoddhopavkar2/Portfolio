On November 26, 2022, I had just landed in the US to start a master's degree. I had one goal: get an internship.

Online assessments and technical interviews stood in the way, so I started doing the LeetCode daily problem. It was a means to an end.

Somewhere along the way it stopped being a chore. It became a ritual, a small thing I did every day whether or not I felt like it. In August 2025 I reached day 1,000.

## What it changed

Mostly, it changed how interviews felt. After a few hundred days I stopped dreading them. I could schedule one on short notice without needing a week to cram, because there was nothing to cram. The practice was already done, a little at a time.

It also gave me something steady during a job market that wasn't. Some weeks nothing else went to plan. The daily problem still got solved.

## What it didn't

The number itself never meant much. A streak counter doesn't make you a good engineer, and a thousand easy problems would have taught me less than a hundred hard ones. The value was in the habit: showing up every day, especially on the days I didn't want to.

## If you're starting

Start smaller than you think you should. One problem a day is enough. Miss a day and start again the next. The point is not the count, it's that tomorrow you'll do it again.
